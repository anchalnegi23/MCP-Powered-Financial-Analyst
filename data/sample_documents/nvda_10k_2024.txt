NVIDIA CORPORATION — FORM 10-K, FISCAL YEAR 2024 (EXTRACT)

ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS

Dependence on a limited number of suppliers and manufacturing partners.
We depend on foundries, memory suppliers and assembly and test partners to manufacture,
assemble, test and package our products. We do not own or operate wafer fabrication
facilities. A substantial majority of our advanced process wafers are manufactured by a
single foundry partner, and interruption of that supply, whether from capacity
constraints, natural disaster, or geopolitical disruption affecting Taiwan, would
materially harm our ability to deliver products and would reduce revenue.

Customer concentration.
Our revenue is concentrated among a small number of customers. Sales to a limited group
of large cloud service providers and system integrators account for a significant portion
of Data Center revenue. The loss of any such customer, a reduction in their capital
spending on accelerated computing infrastructure, or a decision by them to design and
deploy their own accelerators, would materially reduce our revenue.

Export controls and geopolitical risk.
Regulations issued by the U.S. government restrict the export of certain of our advanced
computing products to China and other markets. These controls have reduced our revenue in
affected regions and required us to develop alternative products. Further restrictions,
retaliatory measures, or licensing requirements could reduce demand, increase costs, and
harm our competitive position in a large market.

Competition.
The market for accelerated computing is intensely competitive. We compete with suppliers
of discrete and integrated GPUs, with vendors of custom accelerators including ASICs
developed internally by large cloud providers, and with emerging architectures. Some
competitors have greater financial resources or captive demand.

Demand volatility and inventory risk.
Demand for our products has been volatile. We place non-cancellable orders and prepay for
capacity well in advance of revenue. If demand falls short of expectations, we may hold
excess inventory or incur supply commitment charges, which would reduce gross margin.

ITEM 7. MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS

Revenue for fiscal year 2024 was $60,922 million, up 126% from $26,974 million in fiscal
year 2023. The increase was driven by Data Center revenue growth reflecting strong demand
for accelerated computing platforms used to train and deploy generative AI models.
Gross margin increased to 72.7% from 56.9%, reflecting a favourable product mix weighted
towards Data Center compute and lower inventory provisions than the prior year.
Operating expenses increased 2% as we continued to invest in engineering headcount.
